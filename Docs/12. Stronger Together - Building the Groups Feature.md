# Stronger Together - Building the Groups Feature

There's something magical about working out with others. Even if you're planking alone in your living room at 6 AM, knowing that your coworkers are doing the same thing creates a sense of connection. That's why groups were always central to Plank Challenge's vision.

## The Social Fitness Dilemma

When we first sketched out the groups feature, we faced an interesting design question: How do you balance openness with control? 

Some users want to create a quick, casual group for their weekend hiking buddies. Others want a more curated experience, like a company wellness program where HR needs to approve who joins. And still others want something completely private, shared only with close friends via a secret code.

We ended up with a simple but flexible system: groups can be public or private, and they can have open enrollment or require approval. Four combinations that cover most real-world scenarios.

## The Invite Code Challenge

Private groups needed a way to share access without making them publicly discoverable. Our solution was an 8-character invite code - simple enough to text to a friend, but random enough to prevent guessing.

Here's where attention to detail matters: we deliberately excluded characters that look similar when typed quickly. No zeros (looks like O), no ones (looks like I or L), no letter O (looks like zero). The code "G84ALNAM" is unambiguous whether you're reading it on a phone screen or scribbling it on a napkin.

We also gave group admins the ability to regenerate codes. If an invite leaks to people you didn't want in the group, just create a new code and the old one stops working instantly. It's a small feature, but it gives people peace of mind about sharing their groups.

## Leaderboards: Friendly Competition

The heart of any fitness group is the leaderboard. Ours ranks members by total plank time over different periods - today, this week, this month, or all time.

We wanted the leaderboard to feel motivating, not discouraging. So even if you're ranked 47th out of 50 people, you still see exactly where you stand and how close you are to climbing up. There's always someone just ahead of you to chase.

One subtle design choice: we show your current streak alongside your leaderboard stats. This recognizes that consistency matters as much as raw duration. The person who planks every day for two minutes might be more impressive than someone who does one epic ten-minute plank and disappears.

## Managing the Chaos

Any time you put people together, you need moderation tools. Ours are straightforward but comprehensive.

Group creators automatically become admins. They can promote other members to admin status, giving them the same powers. Admins can approve or deny join requests, remove troublemakers, and even ban users who shouldn't be allowed back.

We made one firm rule: you can't remove or demote the original creator. This prevents a rogue admin from staging a coup and locking everyone out of their own group. The creator always maintains ultimate control.

## What We Learned About Permissions

Building groups taught us a lot about permission systems. The naive approach would be to check "is this user an admin?" before every action. But that quickly gets messy.

Instead, we built small, focused helper functions that express the rules clearly. "Can this user see the member list?" depends on whether it's a private group and whether they're a member. "Can this user remove that member?" depends on whether they're an admin, whether the target is also an admin, and whether they're the creator.

Breaking these down into readable pieces makes the code easier to audit and harder to get wrong. When you're dealing with permissions, clarity beats cleverness every time.

## The Rejoining Edge Case

Here's a bug we almost shipped: what happens when a removed member tries to rejoin?

Our first implementation would fail silently because the database already had a record for that user-group combination. The fix was using an "upsert" operation - if the record exists, update it; otherwise, create it. This handles the rejoining case gracefully.

But we also needed to consider banned users. A ban isn't just a removal - it's a statement that this person shouldn't be able to rejoin, even with a valid invite code. So we check the membership status early and reject banned users before they get anywhere near the join logic.

These edge cases aren't glamorous, but they're the difference between software that mostly works and software you can trust.

## Looking Ahead

With groups in place, Plank Challenge finally feels like a community platform rather than just a personal tracker. But we're not done with competition yet. Next up: global leaderboards where anyone can see how they stack up against the entire user base.

That's a bigger technical challenge - we need to handle much larger datasets efficiently, with real-time updates as people complete their planks. It's time to break out some more advanced infrastructure.

But for now, we're celebrating. Groups are live, and somewhere out there, office mates are challenging each other to plank battles. That's exactly what we set out to build.
