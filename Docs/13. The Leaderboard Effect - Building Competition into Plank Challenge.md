# The Leaderboard Effect: Building Competition into Plank Challenge

*How we added global rankings and laid the groundwork for push notifications*

---

There's something about seeing your name on a leaderboard that changes everything. A solitary habit becomes a shared pursuit. Your personal best becomes a score to defend. That quiet morning plank transforms into a small act of competition with strangers across the globe.

This is the psychology we wanted to tap into with Phase 7 of Plank Challenge.

## The Motivation Question

Before diving into implementation, we had to answer a fundamental question: what actually motivates people to keep planking?

For some, it's purely internal. The satisfaction of a streak. The meditation of holding position. The private victory of lasting ten seconds longer than yesterday.

But for many others, motivation is social. Knowing someone else is out there, pushing through the same burn, creates an invisible thread of connection. Leaderboards make that thread visible.

The research backs this up. Competition, even against strangers we'll never meet, triggers the same reward pathways as direct social interaction. We're wired to compare ourselves to others—not out of insecurity, but because comparison gives us context. Am I doing well? How do I stack up? What's possible?

A leaderboard answers these questions at a glance.

## Four Ways to Compete

We didn't want to create a single leaderboard that only rewarded one type of achievement. Different people excel at different things, and we wanted everyone to have a chance to shine.

So we built four distinct rankings:

**Streak Champions** measure consistency. Who can show up day after day, week after week, without breaking the chain? This rewards the tortoise over the hare—you don't need to be the strongest, just the most persistent.

**Duration Masters** celebrate raw endurance. Who can hold a single plank the longest? This is pure physical achievement, the kind that makes your muscles scream and your mind beg for mercy.

**Volume Leaders** track total planks completed. Maybe you do three quick planks every morning instead of one long one. That counts too. Quantity has a quality all its own.

**Time Accumulators** sum up every second spent in plank position. This combines duration and frequency—the ultimate measure of total effort invested.

Each leaderboard tells a different story. You might be rank 500 in duration but rank 50 in streaks. That's not failure; it's a roadmap showing where your strengths lie and where you might grow.

## The Caching Challenge

Here's a technical reality that every leaderboard faces: they're expensive to calculate.

To show the top 50 users by streak, you need to query every user, sort them, and return the results. Do that every time someone opens the leaderboard, and you're running the same expensive query thousands of times per minute.

The solution is caching. Calculate the leaderboard once, store the results, and serve that stored version for the next sixty seconds. Only when the cache expires do you recalculate.

This creates what we call "eventual consistency." If you complete a plank right now, you might not see your updated rank for up to a minute. For real-time stock trading, that would be unacceptable. For a fitness app leaderboard? Perfectly fine. Nobody's making life decisions based on whether they're rank 47 or rank 48.

The tradeoff is worth it. Fast responses and lower server costs in exchange for slightly stale data that nobody notices anyway.

## Friends vs. Strangers

Global leaderboards are inspiring, but they can also feel intimidating. Seeing someone with a 500-day streak when you're on day 12 might motivate you—or it might make you want to give up entirely.

That's why we added a friends leaderboard.

This shows rankings only among people you follow. Suddenly you're not competing against fitness influencers and professional athletes. You're competing against your college roommate, your sister, that coworker who challenged you to a plank-off last month.

These are manageable comparisons. Achievable goals. If Sarah from accounting has a 30-day streak, maybe you can catch her by next week. That's not a fantasy—it's a plan.

The friends leaderboard supports all four ranking types, so you can compete with your circle in whatever dimension matters most to you.

## Time-Based Competitions

All-time leaderboards have a problem: they're dominated by people who've been using the app the longest. A newcomer with incredible dedication can never catch someone who started a year earlier.

We solved this with period filtering.

Weekly leaderboards reset every seven days. Monthly leaderboards every thirty. This gives everyone a fresh start regularly. You might never crack the all-time top 100, but you could absolutely win this week.

For streaks, the period filter works differently. Instead of showing the longest streak ever achieved during the week (which would still favor long-term users), it shows current active streaks for users who planked within the period. This means the weekly streak leaderboard shows who's actively maintaining their streak right now.

It's a subtle distinction, but it keeps the competition fair and fresh.

## Your Rank, Always Visible

One problem with paginated leaderboards: if you're rank 847, you have to scroll through 16 pages to find yourself. Nobody does that.

So we calculate your rank separately. Even when viewing the top 50, the app shows your current position. "You're rank 847 with a 14-day streak." No scrolling required.

This gives you context without requiring effort. You know where you stand. And if you're close to a milestone—say, rank 100—that visibility might be exactly the motivation you need to push harder.

## Preparing for Push

Phase 7 also laid the groundwork for push notifications. We built the entire device registration system—users can register their phone's notification token, update it when it changes, and unregister when they log out.

The actual sending of notifications requires some additional setup with Apple's push notification service, which we'll tackle in a future phase. But all the infrastructure is ready. When a user earns a badge or their friend beats their streak, we'll be able to ping their phone instantly.

For now, the devices table sits quietly, collecting tokens, waiting for its moment to shine.

## The Security Details

Push notification tokens are sensitive. They're essentially a direct line to someone's phone. If leaked, a malicious actor could spam a user with notifications.

We took several precautions. Tokens are normalized to lowercase to prevent duplicates. They're validated as hexadecimal strings matching Apple's format. And critically, we added a new endpoint for unregistering that keeps the token out of the URL.

Why does the URL matter? Because URLs get logged. They appear in server logs, analytics dashboards, error tracking systems. A token in a URL is a token that's potentially visible in dozens of places it shouldn't be.

The new approach sends the token in the request body, which isn't logged by default. It's a small change that significantly improves security posture.

## The Inevitable Edge Cases

Building leaderboards revealed some fun edge cases we hadn't anticipated.

What happens when two users have the same score? We needed a tiebreaker. We chose "most recently updated"—if you both have 14-day streaks, whoever planked more recently ranks higher. This slightly rewards consistency and recency.

What about users who've been banned from groups or soft-deleted their accounts? They shouldn't appear on leaderboards. We filter for `deleted_at IS NULL` on every query.

What if someone registers the same device token twice? We made the endpoint idempotent—registering an existing token just updates its metadata rather than creating a duplicate.

These edge cases rarely occur, but handling them gracefully is the difference between software that works and software that works well.

## What Competition Teaches Us

There's a philosophical argument against gamification. That by adding scores and rankings, we cheapen the intrinsic value of the activity itself. That we should plank for the joy of planking, not for a number on a screen.

There's truth to that. And for some users, the leaderboard will be irrelevant—they'll ignore it entirely and focus on their personal practice.

But for others, competition is the spark that ignites discipline. The leaderboard isn't replacing intrinsic motivation; it's providing extrinsic motivation until intrinsic motivation develops. Many people who start planking for the competition eventually continue for the benefits they discover along the way.

It's a gateway, not a destination.

## The Bigger Picture

With Phase 7 complete, Plank Challenge now has a full social layer. You can follow friends. Join groups. Compete on global and friends-only leaderboards. See where you rank across four different dimensions of achievement.

The app has evolved from a simple timer to a fitness community. From a tool to a platform.

One phase remains: the final polish before launch. Error handling audits, security reviews, performance optimization. The unglamorous work that separates a prototype from a product.

But that's for next time.

For now, the leaderboards are live. The competition has begun. Somewhere out there, someone is holding a plank a little longer, pushing a little harder, because they know their rank depends on it.

That's the leaderboard effect in action.
